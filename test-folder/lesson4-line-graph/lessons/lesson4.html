Lesson 4: Line graphs

Glossary terms

Independent variable

The variable that is deliberately changed in a fair test.

Dependent variable

The variable that is observed or measured in response.

X-axis

The horizontal axis. It usually shows the independent variable.

Y-axis

The vertical axis. It usually shows the dependent variable.

Coordinate

A pair of values that gives the exact position of one data point.

Trend

The overall pattern or direction shown by the data.

Reasoned prediction

A prediction supported by scientific knowledge or observations.

Supported

When the evidence agrees with a prediction or explanation.

Lesson 4 · Line graphs

Read the Story in Fair-Test Data

Turn measurements in a table into a line graph, then use the pattern to decide what the evidence tells us.

Learning objective

We are learning how to represent fair-test data in a line graph.

Success criteria

  1. I can label each axis with a variable and unit.
  2. I can plot and double-check data at the correct coordinates.
  3. I can describe a trend and decide whether a prediction is supported.

Today’s investigation route

Table → points → line → evidence

The graph does not create new results. It reorganises the measurements so that patterns and relationships are easier to see.

5. Does the Evidence Support the Prediction?

Original prediction

As temperature increases, the balloon circumference will continue to increase because warmer conditions make yeast more active.
Make a decision from the graph

Is this prediction supported by all of the data?

Choose the evidence

Which statement best explains your decision?

A prediction is tested against the whole pattern. Do not decide from only one pair of points.
🔬

Evidence-based conclusion

The prediction is not supported by all the data. Balloon circumference increased from 10°C to 30°C, but decreased at 40°C and 50°C.

“Not supported” does not mean the prediction was silly. It means the results tell us to revise our explanation.
