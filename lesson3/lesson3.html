Lesson 3: Tables

Glossary Terms

Data

The observations or measurements you collect during an investigation. Data can be numbers (e.g. temperature, height) or descriptions (e.g. colour, texture).

Dependent variable

The result that is observed or measured during a fair test experiment.

Independent variable

The one variable changed during a fair test experiment.

Repeat tests

The same test carried out two or more times under exactly the same conditions, to check that your results are consistent.

Table

An organised arrangement of columns and rows used to display data.

Units

The standard amount or portion of something that is measured, e.g. cm for length, kg for weight, minutes for time.

Is Yeast Alive?

Hover over each image to learn more about the activity of yeast and how it helps produce sourdough bread.
1

Dry yeast is sold in packets. Yeast is a tiny living organism that you can only see under a microscope! Even though it is tiny, yeast is alive, and just like other living things, it needs food to survive. The yeast in these packets is dormant. To wake it up you need to give it water, sugar and the right temperature to make it active.

2

Sugar is used by yeast as food to get energy so it can grow and reproduce.

3

When you mix dry yeast and sugar in water it will start to become active. Active yeast produces and releases carbon dioxide gas. This gas can be seen as bubbles in the mixture. The gas bubbles rise to the surface and pop, so the gas escapes the mixture and enters the air around it.

4

Active yeast produces and releases carbon dioxide gas. When added to bread dough, this makes the dough rise. As bread is baked the bubbles of gas get trapped, making holes in the bread so it is light and soft to eat.
