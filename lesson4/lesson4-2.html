Lesson 4 · Bar Graphs

Glossary Terms

Graph

A visual way of showing data so patterns, comparisons and relationships are easier to see.

X-axis

The horizontal axis. In a fair test line graph, this often shows the independent variable — what was changed.

Y-axis

The vertical axis. In a fair test graph, this often shows the dependent variable — what was measured.

Line graph

A graph that uses points and lines to show how a measured value changes across ordered or continuous values.

Bar graph

A graph that uses separate bars to compare amounts across different categories.

Line Graphs

Line graphs help scientists show how one variable changes continuously as another variable changes, making trends and patterns easier to see.

Learning objective

We are learning how to construct and interpret a line graph.

Success criteria

  1. I can describe what the x-axis and y-axis show.
  2. I can plot points on the graph and connect them with a line.
  3. I can describe trends or patterns in line graphs.

Think like a scientist

When do scientists use a line graph?

In a line graph, a line connects the points. The line shows how one variable continuously affects another variable.

1
Look for a continuous variable We use a line graph when the independent variable is continuous and can include values between whole numbers.
2
Think about what can vary continuously Examples include length, weight, temperature, volume and depth.
3
Connect the data points The connected line helps you see trends or patterns as the x-axis variable changes.

How does the distance from the low tide mark affect the number of elephant snails on the rock platform?

Reasoned prediction

As the distance from the low tide mark increases, the number of elephant snails decreases. Elephant snails are large marine snails and risk drying out, so they prefer being close to the water.

Practise Applying the Principles

Testable question

How does temperature affect yeast activity?

Reasoned prediction

As the temperature increases, the activity of the yeast increases. Living things like warmth, so warmth helps yeast to grow.

Yeast in water feed on sugar and produce carbon dioxide gas as they grow and divide. The gas can be captured in a balloon. The size of the balloon indicates how much gas is produced, and therefore how active the yeast are.
